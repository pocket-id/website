Pocket ID is a self-hosted OpenID Connect (OIDC) provider: one place where your users sign in, for every app you connect to it.
It's made for homelabs and small teams that find [Keycloak](https://www.keycloak.org) or [Authentik](https://goauthentik.io) too much for what they need.

Pocket ID only supports [passkeys](https://www.passkeys.io), so there are no passwords to reuse, leak or phish.
A passkey can live in a password manager, on a phone or on a hardware key such as a YubiKey, and it unlocks with a fingerprint, face scan or PIN.

You can try it on the [demo instance](https://demo.pocket-id.org).

## How sign-in works

Any app that offers sign-in with OpenID Connect, often labeled as SSO, OAuth or OIDC, can use Pocket ID.
You register the app in Pocket ID as an **OIDC client**, and the app sends its users to Pocket ID when they sign in:

<SignInFlow />

Pocket ID only lets a user through when their passkey checks out and, if you restricted the client to some groups, when they're a member of one of them.
The app then receives tokens with the user's name, email address and groups, and creates its own session from them.

Apps without OIDC support, and apps that only support LDAP or SAML, can still sit behind Pocket ID with an authentication proxy, as [Proxy services](/docs/guides/proxy-services) shows.

## Concepts

- **Users** sign in with their passkeys, and an admin can let a user in once with a **login code**, such as to add their first passkey.
- **Groups** decide which users may sign in to which client, and the apps you connect receive them in the token to assign roles.
- **OIDC clients** are the apps that use Pocket ID for sign-in, each with its own client ID, secret and callback URLs.
- **APIs** are your own backends that accept access tokens from Pocket ID, which you only need for [API access](/docs/guides/apis).
